FileMind Jul 2026 · organizes files by what’s in them Repo

I built FileMind, a desktop app for anyone whose folders are full of files with names that say nothing. It reads each file, uses an LLM pipeline to design a folder structure from the content, and sorts everything into it, built on Electron, FastAPI, and Python with local embeddings for search. Each file becomes a structured profile indexed with SQLite full-text search and local MiniLM embeddings in ChromaDB, and the LLM steps (a survey, three competing folder proposals, a judge, then assignment and audit) only ever see that table, never the raw files. It can organize a whole drive by meaning, holds sensitive files for review, and every move can be undone.

Python · FastAPI · Electron · React · SQLite FTS5 · ChromaDB · ONNX

RetentionLab Apr 2026 · Bitcamp Devpost

I built RetentionLab for short-form creators who want to know whether a video will hold attention before they post it. It runs the clip through a multimodal pipeline (WhisperX, YOLO, PySceneDetect, CLAP) and an ONNX scoring model that rates hook, retention, trend fit, and novelty, then a CapCut AI agent makes the suggested edits. The extracted features merge into a 64-dimension vector, and after each round of edits the video is re-exported and rescored, with the loop stopping once gains per edit level off. Creators see exactly what’s dragging the video down and get an edited version back automatically.

Four scores and a ranked edit plan within 60 seconds of upload

AudioSight Mar 2026 · YHack Devpost

We built AudioSight to help blind and low-vision people navigate with an iPhone and Ray-Ban Meta glasses, and I led the four-person team. Depth Anything V2 runs on the iPhone’s Neural Engine through Core ML and turns nearby obstacles into directional spatial audio, Gemini Live answers voice questions, and I built the GPS navigation with turn-by-turn checkpoints, off-route rerouting, and obstacle alerts that override directions. Each depth frame is split into six columns and sampled 10 times a second, and route checkpoints come from decoding Google Directions polylines and resampling them along the walking path. Users hear where obstacles are in real time while being guided to their destination.

End-to-end latency 250 ms → ~50 ms with on-device inference

ThirdEye Feb 2026 · DevFest · 1st of 100+ Devpost

I built ThirdEye for readers who get stuck on dense material and for teams whose documents confuse people. A Chrome extension tracks your gaze through the webcam to detect where you’re stuck, and a FastAPI agent pipeline sends that passage to Gemini, which writes an explanation at your level and saves it to Google Docs. Behind the extension, separate agents interpret the passage, hypothesize the reader’s knowledge gap with K2-Think, and compose the explanation, while friction events are stored in Snowflake to power the team view. Readers get help without leaving the page, and teams see which parts of their documents confuse people most.

EyeCU Jan 2026 · Hoya Hacks Devpost

I built EyeCU for hospital staff who need to watch patients at risk of falling or self-harm but can’t be in every room. It runs MediaPipe pose tracking in the browser to flag risky behavior like getting out of bed or slumping, then Gemini confirms it before alerting nurses over WebSockets. The browser model tracks 33 body landmarks to compute leg-dangle ratio and torso angle, and a behavior only counts once it persists across several frames, so one noisy frame can’t set off an alarm. One nurse can monitor many rooms at once, with pose checks under 16 ms per frame and Gemini reviewing only flagged moments.

FrontendReact · TypeScript · Vite · Tailwind CSS · MediaPipe Tasks Vision · Zustand

BackendNode.js · Express · WebSockets · Supabase · Gemini · JWT auth · PDFKit
